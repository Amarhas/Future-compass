# Evidence-Based Translation Layer

Future Compass should translate skills into activities that create observable evidence.

The product should not ask a child to complete random tasks. Each activity should make it clear:

- why the activity matters
- what skill is being practiced
- what behavior counts as evidence
- what proof or artifact is produced
- how a grown-up can support without over-labeling
- what kind of next challenge might fit

## Core model

```text
Research-backed mechanism
↓
Child-friendly mission
↓
Observable behavior
↓
Artifact or proof
↓
Reflection
↓
Adult interpretation
↓
Next recommended challenge
```

## Research-to-product translation

| Research driver | Plain-language meaning | Product translation |
|---|---|---|
| Metacognition and self-regulation | Children benefit when they learn to plan, try, check, and improve their work. | Add Before You Start, What to Notice, Reflection, and one-change improvement prompts. |
| Universal Design for Learning engagement | Children are more likely to engage when tasks feel relevant, purposeful, appropriately challenging, and supported. | Add Why This Matters, Start Here choices, If You Get Stuck, and Level-Up options. |
| Project-based learning | Hands-on tasks help learners create visible evidence rather than only talking about interests. | Add Proof You Made and artifact-based challenges. |
| Career intervention research | Career exploration is stronger when it builds confidence and includes support. | Add real-world connections, next-step rules, and grown-up coaching notes. |
| Work-based learning / career pathways | Career-connected learning should show where skills appear in real work without pressuring a child into a job label. | Add Where This Shows Up and roles that use similar skills. |
| Social-emotional learning | Soft skills should become observable behaviors, such as explaining, helping, persisting, listening, and collaborating. | Add observable evidence prompts and adult interpretation notes. |

## Parent / grown-up research language

The grown-up section should be easy to understand but still fact-based.

Avoid:

> This activity operationalizes metacognitive self-regulation through a constructivist project-based intervention.

Prefer:

> This activity helps your child practice making a first attempt, testing it, noticing what happened, and improving it. Research on learning strategies supports this kind of plan-try-check-improve cycle.

## Child-facing structure

Each scaffolded activity should include:

```text
Mission
Why this matters
Where this shows up
You are practicing
Before you start
What to notice
Proof you made
If you get stuck
Level-up option
Reflection
```

## Grown-up structure

Each parent/adult section should include:

```text
What this activity is really working on
Research-backed why
Evidence to look for
Do not overinterpret
Possible next step
```

## Pilot activity

PR #7 applies this model only to:

```text
Build a Paper Tower
```

The goal is to test the tone and layout before scaling the model to all activities.

## Design rule

The research layer must not make the child-facing app feel scientific, clinical, or like a worksheet.

The child should feel:

> I understand why this tiny challenge matters.

The grown-up should feel:

> I understand what kind of evidence to notice and what not to overinterpret.

## Source anchors

The model is based on the following evidence anchors:

- Education Endowment Foundation: metacognition and self-regulation, high impact with an average of +8 months additional progress.
- CAST Universal Design for Learning: engagement through choice, relevance, value, authenticity, meaningful goals, challenge/support, and action-oriented feedback.
- Zhang & Ma, 2023: project-based learning meta-analysis with positive overall learning effect, random-effects SMD 0.441.
- Whiston et al., 2017: career choice intervention meta-analysis with weighted mean effect size 0.352 and career decision-making self-efficacy average effect size 0.452.
- MDRC Career Academies: random-assignment evaluation showing sustained earnings gains averaging 11 percent, or $16,704 over eight years.
- Durlak et al., 2011: SEL meta-analysis of 213 programs and 270,034 students showing improved skills, attitudes, behavior, and an 11-percentile-point achievement gain.
